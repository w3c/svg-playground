svgwg#1170: where whitespace and percent-encoding are allowed in an svgView() fragment

Open this file in Safari, in Firefox and in Chrome. It measures each row in whichever engine you opened it in and fills the table below. Nothing is pre-recorded: every "accepted" or "rejected" is what your engine did just now.

In plain words. A link into an SVG file can carry a view specification, for example picture.svg#svgView(viewBox(100,0,100,100)), which means "show me that rectangle of the drawing". The specification says in one sentence that spaces are allowed in such a fragment. This page asks, one position at a time, whether they actually are. It also asks how much of the punctuation can be written in percent-encoded form, %28 for ( and so on.

How each row is measured

A 200 by 200 SVG of four solid quadrants is loaded into an img through a blob URL with the fragment appended, drawn into a canvas, and the pixels counted. One dominant colour means the view was applied and only the top-right quadrant is showing. Four distinct colours means the whole fragment was discarded and the untouched drawing is showing. The method is the one from probe-svgview-spaces.html in this folder (2026-08-20).

Rows shaded yellow are new here and have never been measured in any engine: they split whitespace inside the viewBox parentheses into its leading and trailing halves, which the August probe ran only as one combined row. Reading WebKit's and Blink's source predicts leading accepted and trailing rejected in both, and both accepted in Gecko. This page is where that prediction gets checked.
